Dubai Land Department transactions, 2015–2026

CS333 Part 2 (Vega-Lite). Group members: Diana Alkassab, Masa Assaf, Maria Baghdadi, Sana Mathbut, Latifa Almatri. All charts use transactions registered from 1 January 2015 to 31 August 2026. Dates are read and grouped in UTC, so the results are the same in every time zone.

Task 1: Transactions by day of the week

How many transactions were registered on Fridays?
6,553 transactions were registered on Fridays between 1 January 2015 and 31 August 2026. Friday is low mainly because it was part of the UAE weekend until January 2022: there were only 299 Friday transactions in 2015–2021, then 740 in 2022, rising to about 1,750 a year in 2024–2025. No transactions fall on Saturday or Sunday, so both bars are zero, while Monday (20,126) is about 1.5 times any other day, probably because transactions build up over the weekend. Bars on a common baseline make these counts easy to compare, and the zero labels show the empty weekend days explicitly.

Task 2: Monthly sales, off-plan vs existing properties

Which month had the most off-plan sales?
Off-plan sales were highest in September 2025, with 706 sales (existing properties had 316 that month). The next highest months were October 2024 (665) and December 2025 (656). Off-plan sales were higher than existing sales in every month of 2024 and 2025, while April–May 2020 shows the COVID-19 dip for both. A line chart fits because position along a shared time axis shows the trend and where the lines cross, and colour separates the two categories.

Task 3: Median price per m² of units and villas by community

Which community has the largest number of sales of units and villas?
Al Barsha South Fourth (community 681, around Jumeirah Village Circle) has the most sales of units and villas: 3,925 between 1 January 2015 and 31 August 2026, with a median of about 12,900 AED/m². It is followed by Business Bay (3,634) and Marsa Dubai (2,955). This matches Part 1.

Is the map effective for finding it? No.
The map uses colour to show the median price per m², not the number of sales. To find the busiest community, a reader has to hover over every community one by one. Al Barsha South Fourth is also mid-priced (light orange), so nothing makes it stand out, and large communities draw the eye whatever their sales. In Part 1, bubble size showed the number of sales, so the busiest area was easy to spot. A choropleth can use colour for only one measure. A bar chart of sales per community, sorted from highest to lowest, would show the answer at a glance.

How we made the colours reliable.
Communities with fewer than 10 sales are shown in grey rather than coloured, because a median of so few sales is not reliable. For example, Al Barsha South First has a single sale at 3,189 AED/m², and colouring it would wrongly suggest a cheap area. Prices are also skewed: most communities sit between about 10,000 and 25,000 AED/m², but a few reach 75,000, so a continuous scale would make almost all communities look the same. We used six price classes with round boundaries (10k, 13k, 16k, 20k, 30k), which hold between 9 and 17 communities each, and a light-to-dark scale where darker means more expensive. Communities with no unit or villa sales are shown in light grey, so every community stays visible.
